Abstract

Dexterous manipulation depends on how the hand interacts with an object and how the object interacts with its surroundings. Contact pressure provides a representation of these interactions and can guide their transfer from humans to robots. We present ImPress, a framework built around contact pressure for learning dexterous robot manipulation from immersive virtual reality (VR) human demonstrations. First, the ImPress demonstration collection pipeline lets operators demonstrate manipulation tasks directly inside a physics simulator, recording dense contact pressure fields without instrumenting the hands or objects. The resulting ImPress dataset contains 7,100 demonstrations of 71 contact-rich manipulation tasks. Each demonstration records hand-object and, where present, object-object contact pressure, and can be replayed directly in simulation. Second, the ImPress retargeting pipeline uses the recorded pressure to guide where each robot makes contact during model predictive path integral (MPPI) control, without requiring correspondence between human and robot hand parts. Across eight tasks and three robot end effectors, it achieves a success rate of 72.4% compared with 32.0% for the strongest baseline and leads on every task-embodiment pair. Third, the ImPress policy learning pipeline trains diffusion policies on the retargeted trajectories and fine-tunes them with reinforcement learning, using a reward that preserves the robot's contacts from its own successful executions. On test object initial states, contact-guided reinforcement learning improves average success from 32.9% with behavior cloning to 55.7%, versus 52.1% for motion-guided RL, with higher average success on all tasks.

From demonstration to policy

Demonstrate in VR

Operators manipulate objects inside a physics simulator. The system records hand and object motion alongside dense hand-object and object-object contact pressure, without instrumenting hands or objects.

Retarget through contact

Pressure guides grasp initialization and contact adjustments during model predictive path integral (MPPI) control, without requiring correspondence between human and robot hand parts.

Learn and refine

Diffusion policies learn from successful retargeted trajectories. Reinforcement learning then refines them with a reward that preserves contacts from the robot’s own successful executions.

A dataset of contact-rich interactions

7,100 demonstrations across 71 manipulation tasks, with contact pressure and motion that can be replayed directly in simulation.
